1 | The invisible part of the job

Researchers have a name for it, connective labor: the effort of perceiving another person and letting them know they have been perceived. It rarely appears in a job description, and it does not fit neatly on a dashboard. So when an organization looks for something to streamline, it is often the first thing to go.

What replaces it is efficient, and often perfectly pleasant. But efficiency is not the same as recognition, and people can feel the difference long before they can name it.

The question is not whether machines can help. It is what we lose when the moment of being seen is treated as a cost rather than as the point.

2 | Who gets to be seen

Attention has never been evenly shared. Those with money have always been able to buy it: the private tutor, the family doctor who knows your history. As more services are automated, that gap may widen, with a human on the other end becoming a premium feature and everyone else getting the app.

That is why the most expensive service of the future may simply be a person who looks up and sees you.
